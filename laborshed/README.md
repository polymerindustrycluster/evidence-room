# No County Is A Labor Market

**Where do the people who work in each county actually live — and is the answer a
Northeast Ohio problem or a definition of what a metro is?**

Source: LEHD LODES8 origin-destination, 2022 (2019 baseline for the pandemic test),
segment JT00 (all jobs), Ohio main + aux; peer benchmark reads 397 counties in 73
single-state peer metros the same way.

**What a row is:** one (home county, work county) count of JOBS — not people, not
commuters. A two-job worker appears twice. The home end is a residence on file, not
evidence of a trip.

## Read before quoting anything from this page

- **Whole economy, not the cluster.** LODES origin-destination has no industry
  dimension; a polymer-specific version of this page cannot be built from this source.
- **Not a commute.** Nothing here counts a trip. Distant residence (Columbus, Toledo,
  Cincinnati, Dayton) is roughly half the imported workforce and was already in 2019.
- **Denominators move the numbers.** The matrix and dumbbell use the fullest
  denominator; the 397-county benchmark is in-state-only on both sides so peers are
  measured identically (Summit 48.9% there vs 48.4% fully counted; Ashtabula 72.7% vs
  68.5%). The reading line under the benchmark explains the Ashtabula case.
- **One accent, one job.** Orange means "under half" everywhere it appears. Plum is
  the distant-metro series, slate the residents-side dots, dark teal the matched peer
  (Pittsburgh).
- **Mobile summarizes the matrix on purpose.** Below 760px the 13-column matrix
  becomes a per-county three-way split (own residents / rest of PIC-12 / outside);
  the full cell detail stays in the table view. This is an editorial aggregation,
  stated in the figure's source line.
- The vignette ("a quality engineer whose paycheck says Barberton and whose mortgage
  says Columbus") is **illustrative** — a composite consistent with the data, not a
  person in it. The counts beside it (26,960 / 23,506) are real and claim-guarded.

## Reporting that would upgrade the vignette (drafted ask, not yet done)

The human-scale beat currently sits at the composite-vignette rung. One reported
paragraph would beat it. Ask a large Summit County manufacturer's HR or plant manager
(via PIC membership; Bridgestone Akron, Goodyear, or a Barberton molder are natural
calls):

1. How many of your salaried hires since 2020 live outside the twelve counties, and
   where?
2. For those, what does "on site" actually mean per month?
3. Did you post those roles as hybrid/remote, or did the arrangement emerge?

The quote slots into the vignette band in place of the composite sentence; keep the
LODES counts beside it.
