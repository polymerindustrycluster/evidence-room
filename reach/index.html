Polymer research from Akron and Case Western, 2015–2024

The region’s polymer research reaches more than 600 institutions in 50 countries, and four papers in five are led from here, of the 1,222 that name a corresponding author.

That research is published by the region’s two universities, the University of Akron and Case Western Reserve. Between 2015 and 2024 their polymer papers carried co-authors at national laboratories, at a polymer company in Akron, and at universities on five continents. A single collaboration count cannot separate two questions: who does the region bring in, and who brings the region in?

By John Swanson, Polymer Industry Cluster desk · Analysis and graphics by Claude (Anthropic) · Data OpenAlex works and institutions · August 2026

The network

Every institution on a polymer paper with Akron or Case Western

Two rules decide what is counted. The first rule decides which side led a paper: the credit goes to the corresponding author, not the first author, because in this field the first author is normally the graduate student who ran the experiment while the corresponding author is the investigator whose lab it is. The second rule sets the boundary of which papers count as polymer papers, and that boundary is a subject classification rather than a keyword. An earlier version of this page searched for the word “polymer” in the text and returned 4,037 papers. Four of its five largest partners were hospitals doing biomedical polymer research, a different field and a different cluster. Bounding the count instead by OpenAlex’s subject classification for polymers and plastics, the topic label the database assigns to every paper, leaves 1,448. The map draws one mark for each institution at its own location, sized by how many papers it shares with the region. Color says which way the work flowed: teal where a corresponding author sat here, orange where the region joined a project someone else was leading. Institutions that appear on a single shared paper are left off the map and listed in the table under it, because at this scale a one-paper dot is too small to read.

Which direction

The region leads four papers in five of those that name a corresponding author

Reach and standing are different things, and this page counts both. A region that mostly joins other people’s projects travels widely but leads little. A region that mostly leads has standing at home but may not travel. So every major partner carries two counts, papers led from here and papers led from there, and neither count is subtracted from the other.

Whether it lands

A quarter of these papers sit in the top tenth of their field

Collaboration is not impact. Whether the work lands is a separate question, and this section answers it on its own rather than plotting it against the partner counts above. Raw citation counts are not used: a 2015 paper has had a decade to accumulate them and a 2024 paper has had months, so a raw trend would draw the calendar. The measure here is field-weighted citation impact (FWCI), where 1.0 is the world average for a paper of that field and age. The top tenth is a rank OpenAlex gives each paper against others of its own field and year, and 309 of the 1,364 papers that carry a score sit in it.

A cluster whose papers reach 50 countries is more than a regional asset that happens to sit here. The region leads most of that work rather than joining it.
