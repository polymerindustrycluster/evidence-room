Where Northeast Ohio actually sits

Ohio is first in the country in plastics and rubber employment.

That is the sentence PIC can carry into a funder meeting: 54,846 jobs in plastics and rubber products manufacturing, private ownership, 2024, and all 51 state geographies are disclosed, so nothing is missing from that ranking. The metro claims need more care, because the bureau withholds 227 of the country’s 382 metro areas.

By the Polymer Industry Cluster desk · Analysis by Claude (Anthropic), reviewed by a person · Data BLS QCEW annual averages, 2015–2024 · August 2026

The clean claim

The top 20 states, and Ohio is first with no asterisk

States are the one geography the bureau barely suppresses: 51 of 51 disclosed, none withheld. That makes this the most defensible comparison on the page, and the one to lead with in a funder conversation.

Suppressed: the bureau withholds a figure when too few employers exist for it to be published without identifying one of them. The jobs are real; the number is not disclosed, which is not the same as zero. An establishment is one physical site, not one company. Concentration is the region’s share of an industry measured against the country’s: 1.0 is exactly the national share. Private ownership counts private employers only, leaving out federal, state and local government jobs.

The narrow claim

Akron and Cleveland among the metros we can see

Each dot is a metro area: jobs across, concentration up. Seventeen disclosed metros carry 4,000 or more plastics and rubber jobs, and 37 run at twice the national share or better. Seven clear both bars, Akron among them, and Akron is the most concentrated of the six biggest disclosed metros. Cleveland is the other half of the cluster’s story, bigger on jobs and much less concentrated, which is what a large diversified metro looks like in this industry.

Who else is up there

The company Akron keeps is six factory towns

Six other metros share that upper-right corner, and none of them is a big city. Elkhart, Indiana builds most of the country’s recreational vehicles. Hickory, North Carolina makes furniture and fiber-optic cable. Erie, Pennsylvania runs injection molding and mold-making shops. Greenville, South Carolina holds Michelin’s North American headquarters and BMW’s only US assembly plant. Scranton and Greensboro fill out the seven. What those towns make is not in the BLS file: it comes from the companies’ and trade groups’ own public statements, and it is the one passage here we cannot re-check against the data. Akron belongs in that company on the same terms. Its 6,910 jobs sit across 117 establishments, which is a plant of about 59 people, repeated 117 times.
