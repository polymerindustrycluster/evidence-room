Is the Tech Hub award big?

Routine federal contracts bring the region’s polymer industry about $35 million a year.

The EDA Tech Hub award is $51.0 million. Ordinary federal contracting in chemical and plastics manufacturing across the same twelve counties has run at $34.9 million a year since 2019, so the award is worth about a year and a half of money the region was already getting.

Obligation: money the government has legally committed to pay. It is a promise on the books, not cash already spent, and it is the figure federal spending data reports.

$51.0M of Tech Hub money is about a year and a half of what already arrives

Federal obligations to polymer industry codes at place of performance, by fiscal year, in 2025 dollars. FY2026 is a partial year.

Where the work is recorded. Place of performance is a reported field on the award, not an observation of where the work happened. A centrally administered contract, or a prime performing through a subaward, can put the county code somewhere other than the activity. An obligation is also not an outlay: it is money committed, which may be spent across several years or de-obligated.

What it buys

The largest recipient is a category defined by what it is not

The same obligations, broken out by six-digit industry across all eight years and on the same 2025-dollar basis. Two rubber-product codes hold 65% of eight years of money

Federal obligations by six-digit NAICS code, FY2019 to FY2026 combined, in 2025 dollars. Bar labels are shortened; the table carries the full census names.

The chart has two blind spots worth naming. University and research awards file under education and research industry codes, so the NSF NEO-SMART Engine, $15.0 million, and TARDISS are real federal money in these counties that appears in no bar above. And the same query run across all industries rather than polymer codes returns $235.5 billion for the same twelve counties over the same years, nearly all of it aerospace, defense and health contracting with no polymer content. The two scopes sit in one data file and are never added together.

The other instrument

The award money has names on it

The background rate is procurement, and the category view above carries no buyer and no seller, so this page cannot say which firms hold the $51.6 million recorded in fiscal 2019. The award reads down to the project. It went to seven leads, each with a named piece of work: $11.1 million to BioVerde for bio-based butadiene at the top, and $2.7 million at the bottom to stand up a regional office of innovation at the Greater Akron Chamber.

Seven named awards make up the $51.0M line

EDA Tech Hub implementation awards by project lead, in the dollars each was awarded. The rule under each amount is its share of the largest.

A number is big or small only against something.

Routine federal polymer contracting in these twelve counties runs about $34.9 million a year in 2025 dollars. The Tech Hub award, $51.0 million, is about 1.5 years of it, and in fiscal 2019 the routine flow was larger than the whole award on its own. A competitive grant and a procurement obligation are different instruments, so this is a comparison of order of magnitude rather than of like for like.
