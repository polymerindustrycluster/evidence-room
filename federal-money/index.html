Is the Tech Hub award big?

Routine federal contracts bring the region’s polymer industry about $35 million a year.

The EDA Tech Hub award is $51.0 million. Ordinary federal contracting in chemical and plastics manufacturing across the same twelve counties has run at $34.9 million a year since 2019, so the award is worth about a year and a half of money the region was already getting.

By the Polymer Industry Cluster · Analysis and graphics by Claude (Anthropic) · Data USAspending.gov and the PIC funding map · August 2026

The comparison

Set the award beside the money that was already coming

One bar per federal budget year, which runs October to September. These are obligations recorded against chemical (325) and plastics and rubber (326) industry codes, for work performed in the twelve counties. The horizontal line near the top of the chart is the Tech Hub award, drawn at the same scale. In fiscal 2019 the routine flow alone came to $51.6 million, more than the whole award.

Obligation: money the government has legally committed to pay. It is a promise on the books, not cash already spent, and it is the figure federal spending data reports. In 2025 dollars: older years marked up for inflation, so that a 2019 dollar and a 2025 dollar buy the same thing and the eight years can be compared. As awarded is the smaller, unadjusted figure on the original paperwork; the tables carry both.

$51.0M of Tech Hub money is about a year and a half of what already arrives

Each bar is one year of ordinary federal contracting with the region’s polymer industry, in 2025 dollars. A bar that rises past the orange line is a single year worth more than the whole Tech Hub award. The dashed line is the eight-year average. FY2026 is still running.

Where the work is recorded. Place of performance is a reported field on the award, not an observation of where the work happened. A centrally administered contract, or a prime performing through a subaward, can put the county code somewhere other than the activity.

What it buys

The largest recipient is a category defined by what it is not

The same obligations, sorted by industry across all eight years and on the same 2025-dollar basis. Every business in federal data is filed under a six-digit industry number, the census code (NAICS), and the one at the top of this chart, “all other rubber product manufacturing,” is the leftovers class for rubber goods that fit none of the named ones. It takes more of the eight years than any named industry does.

Two rubber-product codes hold 65% of eight years of money

Federal obligations by six-digit industry code, FY2019 to FY2026 added together, in 2025 dollars. Each bar is one industry’s eight-year total, not a single year. Bar labels are shortened; the table carries the full census names.

The chart has two blind spots worth naming. University and research awards file under education and research industry codes, so the NSF NEO-SMART Engine, $15.0 million, and TARDISS are real federal money in these counties that appears in no bar above. And counting every industry in these twelve counties, rather than the polymer codes alone, gives $235.5 billion over the same years, nearly all of it aerospace, defense and health contracting with no polymer content. That is a count of something else, hundreds of times larger, and the two are never added together.

The other kind of money

The award money has names on it

The routine flow is the government buying things, and it reaches this page as industry categories with no buyer and no seller attached, so nothing here can say which firms hold the $51.6 million recorded in fiscal 2019. The award reads down to the project. It went to seven leads, each with a named piece of work: $11.1 million to BioVerde for bio-based butadiene at the top, and $2.7 million at the bottom to stand up a regional office of innovation at the Greater Akron Chamber.

Seven named awards make up the $51.0M line

EDA Tech Hub implementation awards by project lead, in the dollars each was awarded. The bar under each amount is drawn against the largest award, so a bar half as long is an award about half the size.

A number is big or small only against something.

Routine federal polymer contracting in these twelve counties runs about $34.9 million a year in 2025 dollars. The Tech Hub award, $51.0 million, is about 1.5 years of it, and in fiscal 2019 the routine flow was larger than the whole award on its own. A competitive grant and a routine purchase order are two different kinds of money, so what is compared here is rough size, not like for like.
