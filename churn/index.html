Hires and separations, 2012–2025

The region recorded 111,500 hires to end up with 168 more jobs.

Every employment chart PIC publishes shows a stock — how many jobs exist. Underneath that flat line, plastics and rubber manufacturing in these twelve counties has been starting and ending roughly a fifth of itself every year for fourteen years. Two things this page will not let you say: these are hire and separation events, not people — one worker hired three times is three hires — and summing twelve counties counts a move from Summit to Stark as both a separation and a hire. The flow is large. It is not 111,529 individuals.

The chart

Hires above the line, separations below, for 55 quarters

Both are flows — jobs starting and ending during the quarter. The line through the middle is the net, which is what a conventional employment chart shows on its own.

The rate

Turnover has risen since 2012 and has not come back down

Churn rate is the average of hires and separations over the jobs that exist — the share of the workforce that moved in or out that quarter. The pandemic spike is visible, but so is the fact that the line never returns to where it started.
