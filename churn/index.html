Job flows against headcount · Census QWI, 2012–2025

A tenth of this workforce turns over every quarter. Almost none of it is growth.

Two Census measures answer two different questions. Are there more jobs? No: the headcount fell by 719, to 17,725. How much movement is under that? Plastics and rubber here starts about a tenth of itself and ends another tenth every quarter, and the flow ledger nets them: 111,529 hires, 111,361 ends, +168. They are job events, not people: a Summit-to-Stark move counts twice.

Is a tenth of the jobs, every quarter, a lot? Nothing here answers that against another industry or another state: this pull carries no second series to hold it against. What it can answer is which way the movement runs, and that is the half that decides whether churn is good news. A payroll churns when people leave for better work, and it churns when a plant cannot keep anyone. Since 2012 the leaving rate has risen 1.9 points while the hiring rate rose 0.2, and since 2023Q1 more jobs have ended than started in every quarter. Of the two readings, that is the worse one.

The flow

Hires above the line, separations below, for 55 quarters

Both are flows: jobs starting and ending during the quarter, counted by the Quarterly Workforce Indicators, a Census series. Their difference is the net flow, the line. A conventional employment chart shows the headcount instead, estimated separately. What follows is how big the movement is, which way it has turned since 2012, and what it looks like on a 150-person floor.

The rate

Turnover has risen since 2012 and has not come back down

Churn rate is the share of jobs that start or end in a quarter: at 10%, about one job in ten starts and another one in ten ends. Higher means more movement through the same payroll, not a gain or loss of jobs. Three readings of it run through this page, and they are not in conflict: 10.8% is the average across all 55 published quarters, 8.5% is where 2012 sat, and 9.6% is the last four published quarters. The pandemic spike is visible, and so is the fact that the line settles above where it started.

The yearly average ends at 9.6%, about 1.1 points above 2012

Which side moved

The extra turnover is people leaving

The rate above averages two things, jobs starting and jobs ending, and only one of them moved. Between 2012 and the last four published quarters the leaving rate rose about 1.9 points while the hiring rate rose about 0.2. In 2012 hiring ran 0.9 points ahead of leaving; averaged over a year, leaving has run ahead in every quarter since 2023Q1.

By county

Where the turnover concentrates

A county’s rate rides on a base, meaning the number of jobs it has to move. A high rate on a small base is a different problem from a high rate on a large one, so this reads on two axes at once: the rate a county runs, and the base it runs it on. Twelve counties are twelve readings of the same measure by the same method, and this is the only place on the page where one place is measured against another.

Trumbull runs the highest rate on the smallest base: 434 starts and ends in a year, on 402 jobs

On a shop floor

What 9.6% a quarter looks like at a 150-person plant

Over the last four published quarters the cluster moved 9.6% of its jobs in a quarter. At a 150-person plant that is about 14 people starting and 14 leaving every quarter, roughly five of each in a month, and about 58 replacement hires to make in a year. Across all 55 quarters the rate averages 10.8%, which works out to 16 and 16 a quarter and about 65 replacement hires a year. The plant is illustrative: it applies a cluster average to a single site, and no single plant runs at the average.

plastics or rubber plant, any of the twelve counties

Flat is what fast looks like from a distance.

Between 2012Q1 and 2025Q3 the cluster recorded 222,890 hire and separation events and finished 168 starts ahead of ends: about 1,327 events for every one job of that margin. That margin is a ledger of flows, and it is not the answer to whether the industry has more jobs. The headcount is, and the headcount fell by 719, about 4%. Net employment is a legitimate outcome measure, and funders are entitled to score it. The point is that a program changing how people move through this industry can do real work without moving that number, and that since 2023Q1 the movement has run toward the door.
