Akron and Case Western, 2012–2025

208 papers since 2012 name both Akron and Case Western. Eight are classified in polymers.

The University of Akron and Case Western Reserve University are counted here two independent ways, by the papers that name both and by the NSF grants they hold together, across the fourteen years from 2012 to 2025. In those years 208 papers named both universities, of which eight are classified in polymers, and the two hold three joint NSF projects worth $3.4 million, the newest of the projects starting in 2017. Papers naming both peaked at 29 in 2018, dropped to 11, and came back to 22 in 2020. It then fell in each of the four years to 2024, down to two papers, and rose to six in 2025.

By John Swanson, Polymer Industry Cluster desk · Analysis and graphics by Claude (Anthropic) · Data OpenAlex works and institutions, NSF Awards API · August 2026

Correction, September 28, 2026: this page previously called the award data “federal awards”, but the fetch queries the NSF Awards API only. The four awards are four joint NSF projects (eight award IDs, since NSF splits a “Collaborative Research” project into one award per institution); no other federal agency is checked. On September 29 four became three, and eight award IDs six; see the correction under the awards chart.

The record

Two hundred and eight papers, and a fall that bottoms out in 2024

Every work in a public index that lists both universities among its author affiliations. Each small mark at the foot of a column is one paper. The lower marks are papers classified under polymers and plastics; the marks above them are papers classified under biomaterials, a separate subject that is counted alongside it and never merged into it. Both are subject classifications the index assigns to a paper, not a match on the word polymer in its text.

Why this page exists

This page replaces an assertion that was never checked: that nothing in the data suggested the polymer chairs at Akron and Case Western, the professors who head each university’s polymer program, had ever been in the same room. That was an unbounded negative, a claim that something has never happened, resting on data that was never about collaboration, and it has been deleted. What the Polymer Industry Cluster (PIC), an initiative of the Greater Akron Chamber, actually has to work with is measured below.

The years counted here stop at 2025, and a large joint award started after they do. Case Western leads NEO-SMART, a regional innovation engine funded by the National Science Foundation, which awarded it on 13 July 2026. The University of Akron is among its core partners, and one of its three research clusters is polymers. That award falls outside this series and is not evidence against it. A reader who already knows about it should not have to wonder whether this page knows about it too.

What this cannot show is an absence. Publication databases and grant records do not systematically capture industry contracts, consortium membership, joint teaching or conversations. These counts therefore cannot measure all forms of collaboration.

The control

Papers naming both fell as a share of Akron’s output, too

A falling count at the end of a series has two innocent explanations before it has an interesting one: the database has not finished indexing recent years, or one partner is simply publishing less of everything. Each university’s total provides context, but cannot rule out delays in indexing papers naming both. The lower panel addresses the second one by dividing the count of papers naming both by Akron’s own output for that year. Read the result as how many papers name both universities for every thousand papers Akron publishes: a higher rate means papers naming both are a larger share of Akron’s indexed output, and a lower rate means a smaller share.

The money

Three joint NSF projects, none starting after 2017 in this window

NSF projects are the second, independent instrument. They are counted two ways, because the National Science Foundation encodes a partnership two ways: as one award whose principal investigator (PI) sits at one university and whose co-principal investigator (co-PI) sits at the other, and as a “Collaborative Research” project split into a separate award for each. Missing the second method undercounts badly. One of the three projects it finds is Northern Ohio AGEP-T, an alliance for graduate education in biology, chemistry and engineering. The series ends in 2025. The NEO-SMART award described above was made in 2026, later than anything this chart can see, and its absence here says nothing about it.

Correction, September 29, 2026: this page counted four joint NSF projects worth $3.7 million, eight award IDs. It is three, worth $3.4 million, six award IDs. The fourth, “Collaborative Research: Algorithm and Theory for Interface Computations” ($257,950), was two awards with the same sole principal investigator, Lingxing Yao: 1620198 at Case Western (July 2016 to September 2018) and 1852597 at Akron (August 2018 to September 2022). That is one researcher moving between the universities, not the two working together; the project’s partner award is at the University of Minnesota. A pair of awards sharing a PI is no longer counted. The newest joint project still starts in 2017.

The question was never whether they have met. It is whether they still do.
