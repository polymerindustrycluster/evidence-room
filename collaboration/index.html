Akron and Case Western, 2012–2024

Akron and Case Western have written 201 papers together since 2012. Seven were about polymers.

Two independent instruments, publications and federal awards, measured over thirteen years. 201 coauthored papers, seven classified in polymers, four joint federal awards worth $3.7 million, none starting after 2017 in this window. Joint output held near its high through 2021, then fell three years running.

By John Swanson, Polymer Industry Cluster desk · Analysis and graphics by Claude (Anthropic) · Data OpenAlex works and institutions, NSF Awards API · August 2026

Two hundred and one papers, and a fall that starts in 2022

Every work in a public index that lists both universities among its author affiliations. Each tick at the base is one paper classified in polymers and plastics, or in biomaterials next to it, a subject classification, not a keyword. They are drawn as individual ticks rather than a proportional band because there are few enough that a band would render as a sliver and read as zero.

Why this page exists

An earlier page here said PIC could put the two polymer chairs in the same room, “which nothing in this data suggests has happened.” That was an unbounded negative resting on data that was never about collaboration, and it has been deleted. This page is what replaces it: the claim, measured. The window closes before the largest relevant award exists. Case Western leads the NEO-SMART NSF Engine, awarded 14 July 2026, and the University of Akron is among its core partners; one of its three research clusters is polymers. The largest joint federal undertaking these two institutions have entered began eighteen months after this window closed. It is outside this series and is not evidence against it, but a reader who knows about it should not have to wonder whether this page does. What this cannot show is an absence: publication databases and grant records do not see industry contracts, consortium membership, joint teaching or a conversation, so a low count here means not visible at this resolution.

The control

It is not indexing lag, and it is not only that Akron shrank

A falling count at the end of a series has two innocent explanations before it has an interesting one: the database has not finished indexing recent years, or one partner is simply publishing less of everything. Both are checkable, and both are drawn here rather than argued in a footnote.

The money

Four joint federal awards, none starting after 2017 in this window

Federal awards are the second, independent instrument. They are counted two ways, because the National Science Foundation encodes a partnership two ways: as one award naming investigators at both institutions, and as a “Collaborative Research” project split into a separate award for each. Missing the second method undercounts badly. The series ends in 2024. An award made after that (the CWRU-led NEO-SMART NSF Engine, July 2026, in which Akron is a core partner) is later than anything this chart can see, and its absence here says nothing about it.

The question was never whether they have met. It is whether they still do.
