PIC accountability · award register as of 13 August 2026

PIC reports securing $106.3 million. $4,149,515 names its own organisation.

PIC, the Polymer Industry Cluster, is an initiative of the Greater Akron Chamber. The two award lines behind that $4,149,515 name the Chamber: 4.9 percent of the $85,335,784 awarded. Another $51.9 million names a firm or university doing the work. The last $29.3 million names a programme, a building, or nobody yet.

By John Swanson, Polymer Industry Cluster desk · Analysis and graphics by Claude (Anthropic) · Data from the PIC award register, the public event register, USAspending and BLS · August 2026

The subtraction the funding map already publishes

PIC holds 4.9 percent of the $85.3 million awarded, and convenes the rest

Three public awards total $85,335,784. Partner match and state cost share, promised by other organisations, add $20,954,667. Together the two sums are the $106.3 million PIC reports securing.

Each stage is a subset of the one above it, and the last is $4,149,515

All bars share one dollar scale. The detached bar is the match, never part of the total.

What this chart licenses. The finding is $4.15 million of governance money sitting underneath the $75,020,661 on award lines naming other recipients. Reading it as “PIC only got $4 million” inverts what the chart shows: the awards commit the funders to pay those other recipients directly, by design, and PIC cannot mis-spend money that never passes through it.

The three public awards are a Tech Hub award from EDA, the federal Economic Development Administration; an Ohio Innovation Hub grant from the state; and APEX, a smaller federal workforce grant. Of their $85,335,784, $79,170,176 is assigned, meaning it sits on an executed award line: a line in a signed agreement that names the organisation holding the money. There are twenty-seven such lines, naming twenty-one recipients. The two naming the Greater Akron Chamber carry $4,149,515: $2,650,000 from EDA under award ED24HDQ0G0413 for innovation governance, and $1,499,515 from the state grant for hub administration. The Tech Hub money arrives as seven separate EDA awards to seven project leads. The Chamber is one of those seven; the other six awards never pass through it.

Awarded, assigned, paid out

92.8 percent of the awards are assigned, and no public record says what has been paid

An award register records commitment and execution. It records no payment. The Tech Hub award and APEX are each fully assigned. The entire unassigned balance, $6,165,608, sits in the Ohio Innovation Hub grant, which is 80.3 percent assigned, and it is held in two workstreams, the named programmes inside the state grant.

The third stage is empty because no public record fills it

Three stages of the same $85,335,784, drawn to one dollar scale. Longer is more money. The dashed outline is the payment stage with no figure in it: no public record carries one, and nothing on this page estimates one.

Two qualifications sit on that chart and belong in the reading of it. The first: the $6,165,608 unassigned balance is two gaps, $3,523,500 and $2,642,108, and only the second is a real gap. Of the $3,748,500 startup-support workstream, $225,000 is assigned as the nine cash awards to the Synthe6 startup cohort. The rest of that workstream is delivered through a sub-grant to Bounce Innovation Hub, the organisation that runs the whole programme. Publishing “$3,523,500 unassigned” without that sentence would manufacture a problem PIC does not have. The other gap, $2,642,108 of the $5,992,000 PIC Translational R&D allocation, is genuinely not yet sub-granted. The second qualification: 29.2 percent of the assigned money names a building and a programme rather than a company, the Polymer Pilot Facility at $18,521,985 and regional workforce programs at $4,572,371. The facility’s groundbreaking is a target dated 9 October 2026 and its readiness a promise dated 15 November 2027. The largest single assigned line, in other words, is money assigned to a building that does not exist yet.

The funding map carries the mechanism behind every one of these lines. This page does not re-explain it.

The coalition of record

Eleven award lines carry 95.5 percent of the money; the other sixteen total $3,574,892

Every name in this table sits in a signed federal Notice of Award, an executed state grant agreement, or a dated public announcement. Each row is one executed award line, so an organisation holding two awards appears twice and its two roles are shown separately.

Eleven lines of $1,000,000 or more hold $75,595,284 of the $79,170,176 assigned

One row per executed award line, largest first. The rule, the horizontal dividing line drawn across the table, falls at $1,000,000: eleven lines above it, sixteen below. Sort by any column heading; the reading below describes the default order. The label on each row says what kind of participation the line records, so a $25,000 cohort award and an $18.5 million capital line are not read as the same thing.

The seven PIC Translational R&D sub-grants total $3,349,892 with a median of $495,000, a largest of $678,614 to the University of Akron and a smallest of $292,500 to Aeris Biosciences. The nine Synthe6 cohort awards are $25,000 each. An operator at an independent firm reading for a number they could win should read the sixteen lines below the rule and not the eleven above it.

What PIC signed up for, in numbers, with dates

Eighteen dated commitments, and only one carries a numeric target

A commitment enters this register when it has a date, an owner and a source document. Eighteen do. Ten resolve by the end of 2026 and eight fall between 2027 and 2029. Exactly one carries a numeric outcome target, and EDA set it in a Notice of Award rather than PIC setting it for itself.

Eighteen dated commitments, PIC-owned above the line and partner-owned below it

One mark per commitment on a date axis; further right is later. A hollow ring means still scheduled and a solid disc means delivered, the same encoding the public event register uses, so the two pages cannot drift apart.

Every row names its owner because a target moved by the state board that controls a grant is that board’s slip, not PIC’s. PIC-owned rows and partner-owned rows are grouped apart, and the PIC-owned ones are shown without hedging. The register also carries one standing rule: only a date that is already public can enter it. Without that rule, a promise register would give PIC a reason to stop publishing forward dates, because every new date is a new way to be caught missing one. With it, publishing a date can never be worse than withholding one.

The programme PIC has described three ways

PIC has published three counts of its R&D competition and they do not reconcile

On 1 July 2025 PIC recorded a first funding cycle of 39 proposals with 14 funded and about $2.1 million in seed awards. On 12 December 2025 PolyKinetix was described as selected “5 of 38”, a phrase that could mean ranked fifth of 38 or one of five funded from 38. On 7 July 2026 the 2026 intake was recorded at 59 applications, up 51 percent year on year. The award register shows seven executed sub-grants totalling $3,349,892. No public document states which population each of those figures counted.

Each row is a number PIC or its award documents have published, the date it was published, and the document it came from. The last column reads “not stated” wherever no published document says.

This page publishes no funded rate for the competition. A stated rate becomes an implied promise that an unsuccessful applicant quotes back, and the denominators are exactly the thing that is unresolved. When the programme office states what each figure counted, the last column fills and the table gets stronger without changing shape.

The negative space

PIC has set itself no targets and assigned no owners

PIC’s internal board scorecard is a separate list from the eighteen commitments above, and the matching count is coincidence: it also holds eighteen rows, fifteen of which PIC answers for. Three carry a target and all three are ceilings fixed by signed award documents. No target on that page was set by PIC, and no row names an owner. That is a governance fact about the organisation rather than a judgment about anyone’s performance, and it has a property worth keeping: the moment PIC sets a target, this page changes, and the change is legible as progress.

Three targets, all of them ceilings somebody else wrote

Generated from the board’s own file. A target cell showing a figure is a ceiling fixed by a signed award document; every other target cell reads “not set”.

One comparison belongs here in prose and gets no chart. The federal Tech Hub award is $51,001,413. For scale, routine federal obligations to polymer firms in the twelve counties PIC covers ran about $34.9 million a year from FY2019 to FY2026, in constant 2025 dollars. A competitive implementation grant and a procurement obligation are different instruments that share only a unit of account, so this page states the two figures side by side and computes no ratio.
