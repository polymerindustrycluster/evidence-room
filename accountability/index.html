PIC accountability · award register as of 13 August 2026

Of the $106.3 million PIC reports securing, $4,149,515 sits on award lines naming PIC’s own organisation.

The rest is obligated straight to other named recipients: six more EDA project leads, and five state workstreams inside one grant. This page publishes that decomposition, the eighteen dated commitments PIC now carries, and the seven rows it answers for that no public record can fill.

By the Polymer Industry Cluster · Analysis and graphics by Claude (Anthropic), directed and edited at the Greater Akron Chamber innovation desk · Data from the PIC award register, the public event register, USAspending and BLS · August 2026

The subtraction the funding map already publishes

PIC holds 4.9 percent of the money it reports securing, and convenes the rest

Three public awards total $85,335,784. Partner match and state cost share add $20,954,667, and those are promises made by different organisations, so this page never sums the two into one bar.

Each stage is a subset of the one above it, and the last is $4,149,515

Dollars, all bars on one scale. Read down: money reported secured, money awarded, money that has reached a named recipient, money on a line naming the Greater Akron Chamber. The bar at the bottom is detached because match is a promise made by other organisations and is never part of the total above it.

What this chart licenses. The finding is $4.15 million of governance money sitting underneath the $75,020,661 that has reached other named recipients. Reading it as “PIC only got $4 million” inverts what the chart shows: the other six EDA awards were designed to bypass the Chamber, and PIC cannot mis-spend money that never passes through it.

Of the awards, $79,170,176 sits on twenty-seven executed lines naming twenty-one recipients. Of that, $4,149,515 is on the two lines naming the Greater Akron Chamber: $2,650,000 from EDA under award ED24HDQ0G0413 for innovation governance, and $1,499,515 from the state grant for hub administration. The other six EDA awards do not pass through it.

Awarded, assigned, disbursed

92.8 percent of the awards have reached a named recipient, and no public record says what has been paid

An award register records commitment and execution. It records no payment. EDA is fully assigned and APEX is fully assigned; the entire unassigned balance, $6,165,608, sits in the Ohio Innovation Hub at 80.3 percent, held in two workstreams.

The third stage is empty because no public record fills it

Three stages of the same $85,335,784, drawn to one dollar scale. Longer is more money. The dashed outline is the absence of a figure rather than a small one, and nothing on this page estimates what belongs in it.

Two qualifications sit on that chart and belong in the reading of it. Of the $3,748,500 startup-support workstream, $225,000 is the nine Synthe6 cohort cash awards and the balance is delivered through the Bounce sub-grant that runs the whole workstream, so publishing “$3,523,500 unassigned” without that sentence manufactures a problem PIC does not have. The other gap, $2,642,108 of the $5,992,000 PIC Translational R&D allocation, is genuinely not yet sub-granted. And 29.2 percent of the money with a “named recipient” names a building and a programme: the Polymer Pilot Facility at $18,521,985 and regional workforce programs at $4,572,371. The facility’s groundbreaking is a target dated 9 October 2026 and its readiness a promise dated 15 November 2027, so the largest single line in the assignment test is money assigned to a building that does not exist yet.

The funding map carries the mechanism behind every one of these lines. This page does not re-explain it.

The coalition of record

Eleven award lines carry 95.5 percent of the money; the other sixteen total $3,574,892

Every name in this table sits in a signed federal Notice of Award, an executed state grant agreement, or a dated public announcement. Each row is one executed line, so an organisation holding two awards appears twice and its two roles are shown separately.

Eleven lines of $1,000,000 or more hold $75,595,284 of the $79,170,176 that has reached a recipient

One row per executed award line, largest first. The rule falls at $1,000,000. Sort by any column heading; the reading below describes the default order. The chip on each row says what kind of participation the line records, so a $25,000 cohort award and an $18.5 million capital line are not read as the same thing.

The seven PIC Translational R&D sub-grants total $3,349,892 with a median of $495,000, a largest of $678,614 to the University of Akron and a smallest of $292,500 to Aeris Biosciences. Nine Synthe6 cohort awards are $25,000 each. An operator at an independent firm reading for a number they could win should read those two rows and not the eleven above the rule.

What PIC signed up for, in numbers, with dates

Eighteen dated commitments, and one of them carries a number that can be missed

A commitment enters this register when it has a date, an owner and a source document. Eighteen do. Ten resolve by the end of 2026 and eight fall between 2027 and 2029. Exactly one carries a numeric outcome target, and EDA set it in a Notice of Award rather than PIC setting it for itself.

Eighteen dated commitments, PIC-owned above the line and partner-owned below it

One mark per commitment on a date axis. A hollow ring with a centre pip is scheduled and a solid disc is delivered, the same encoding the public event register uses, so the two pages cannot drift apart. Further right is later.

Owner attribution is on every row because a target that moves for a state controlling board is not PIC’s slip. PIC-owned rows and partner-owned rows are grouped apart, and the PIC-owned ones are shown without hedging. The register also carries one standing rule, because without it a promise register creates a reason to stop publishing forward dates: only a date already published is eligible, so publishing a date can never be worse than withholding one.

The programme PIC has described three ways

PIC has published three counts of its R&D competition and they do not reconcile

On 1 July 2025 PIC recorded a first funding cycle of 39 proposals with 14 funded and about $2.1 million in seed awards. On 12 December 2025 PolyKinetix was described as selected “5 of 38”. On 7 July 2026 the 2026 intake was recorded at 59 applications, up 51 percent year on year. The award register shows seven executed sub-grants totalling $3,349,892. No document in this repository states which population each of those counted.

Each row is a number PIC or its award documents have published, the date it was published, and the document it came from. The last column reads “not stated” wherever no shipped document says.

This page publishes no funded rate for the competition. A stated rate becomes an implied promise that an unsuccessful applicant quotes back, and the denominators are exactly the thing that is unresolved. When the programme office states what each figure counted, the last column fills and the table gets stronger without changing shape.

The negative space

PIC has set itself no targets and assigned no owners

The internal board scorecard records eighteen rows, fifteen of which PIC answers for. Three carry a target and all three are ceilings fixed by signed award documents. No target on that page was set by PIC, and no row names an owner. That is a governance fact about the organisation rather than a judgment about anyone’s performance, and it has a property worth keeping: the moment PIC sets a target, this page changes, and the change is legible as progress.

Three targets, all of them ceilings somebody else wrote

Generated from the board’s own file. A target cell showing a figure is a ceiling fixed by a signed award document; every other target cell reads “not set”.

Six things this page cannot publish, and what each would need

Generated from the shipped files that carry each limitation, so a hand-maintained honesty list cannot go stale here. Each line carries the date it was defined and either a date it will be filled or a permanent reason.

One comparison belongs here in prose and gets no chart. The Tech Hub award is $51,001,413, against a background rate of about $34.9 million a year in routine federal obligations to regional polymer firms across the same twelve counties, FY2019 to FY2026, in constant 2025 dollars. A competitive implementation grant and a procurement obligation are different instruments that share only a unit of account, so this page states the level and the limit and computes no ratio.
