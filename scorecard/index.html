PIC’s own scorecard · measurement coverage, August 2026

Public records fill eight of the fifteen rows PIC answers for, and none of the four on membership and revenue.

The Polymer Industry Cluster (PIC), an initiative of the Greater Akron Chamber, has won $85.3 million in public grant money, and almost all of it already has a named recipient. The award register names one for $79.2 million of it, and puts $73.2 million of that on an executed line, one with a public record of its execution. The other $6.0 million is Huntsman’s EDA award, signed but with no USAspending record. The register records commitment rather than payment: the federal ledger fills part of that stage, and no public figure covers the whole of it. The four rows that would tell a board whether PIC can pay for itself are measured in PIC’s membership register and general ledger, and this public repository carries neither.

By John Swanson, Polymer Industry Cluster desk · Analysis and graphics by Claude (Anthropic) · Data from the PIC award register, IPEDS and BLS · August 2026 · Federal context updated by Codex (OpenAI), 8 September 2026

One square is one metric. Filled squares are the rows a public record can fill, outlines are the rows whose numbers live only in PIC’s own registers, and the grey run at the end is cluster context PIC does not control.

Why seven rows are empty

Every figure on this page but one is built from public federal and state records and PIC’s published award register. The exception is the member count below, which comes from PIC’s published membership and its own company register. None of it is an account of PIC’s finances.

This repository is public and its history is permanent. The one per-company membership fact it carries is the chain page’s published-member flag, which marks, counts and exports 38 of the 710 companies listed there as members PIC has published; it is not a member list, and an unflagged company is one this repository records nothing about either way. Beyond that flag the tree carries no membership register, and no applicant or personal data. Seven rows below are therefore unpopulated on purpose: their measurement lives in PIC’s membership register, general ledger and drawdown records, and in the PIC and APEX program records. Each one is published here with its definition, cadence and target field ready, and with a visible empty state instead of a figure. Filling them needs a copy of this page kept outside a public repository, and nothing on this page should be read as an estimate of what those cells would say.

The scorecard

Seven of the fifteen rows PIC answers for are blank

One row per metric, in the four groups a board reads in order. Groups A, B and C are what PIC is accountable for: whether it can pay for itself, whether the award money it won reaches the organisations it was won for, and whether the region trains the graduates the cluster hires. Group D is the regional economy PIC works on and does not control, kept at the bottom, set apart, and never averaged into anything above it.

Eight rows compute from public records, seven wait on PIC’s own registers, three are context

A blank cell reads “not published here” and never reads zero. The coloured line at each row’s left edge repeats that status, so it does not rest on the cell alone.

What the target and owner columns mean. No target here was set by PIC. A target showing a figure is a ceiling fixed by a signed award document; every other target reads “not set”, and that absence is the finding rather than a gap in the build. No owner cell names a person, because this repository holds no owner assignment for any row, and under the Entrepreneurial Operating System (EOS), the management framework this scorecard follows, a row without a named owner is not yet a scorecard row.

The groups exist to hold one distinction. The scorecard measures what PIC itself is accountable for. Cluster health measures the regional polymer economy, which PIC influences through award money, convening and programs but does not control. The two must never be averaged or scored on one line. Letting organisational growth stand in for regional outcomes, or regional growth stand in for organisational performance, is the standard way economic-development reporting goes wrong: the cluster adds jobs for reasons no association caused, and the association reports them as delivery.

Group B · award delivery

Every award dollar without a named recipient sits inside one state grant

The register carries three public awards. Two of them are fully assigned, meaning every dollar has a named recipient. The award from the federal Economic Development Administration (EDA) is split into seven implementation awards, each running straight to the project lead that will spend it, and the APEX award, the region’s Good Jobs Challenge workforce grant, is assigned in a single line to regional workforce programs, a programme rather than an organisation. One of the seven EDA awards, $5,970,805 to Huntsman, has no USAspending record, so its execution is not verified. Everything still unassigned is Ohio money, in two workstreams. The PIC Translational R&D balance has not been sub-granted yet. The startup-support balance is delivered through Bounce Innovation Hub under a sub-grant agreement dated 15 May 2025, but the register names recipients in that workstream only for the nine $25,000 cohort awards.

$6.2M of the $85.3M awarded has no named recipient yet, and all of it is Ohio money

One bar per public award, drawn to the same dollar scale. The solid part is money already assigned, with its recipient named on a signed line, though Huntsman’s $6.0M in the EDA bar is not verified as executed. The hatched part is state-grant money with no recipient named yet; the state grant’s grantee is the Greater Akron Chamber, of which PIC is an initiative. Each figure is rounded to the nearest tenth of a million dollars, so the two parts do not add exactly to the total.

One quantity on that chart has no figure that covers the whole of it: how much of the money has actually been paid. An obligation is a legal commitment to pay, and an executed sub-grant is a signed agreement to pay. Neither is a payment. Part of the payment stage is public. USAspending publishes an outlay on seven of the eight federal lines in this register, $11,642,402 of $48,114,979, which is 24.2 percent of their value. Those award lines account for 56.4 percent of the $85.3 million counted here. The $31,250,000 state grant is not a federal award and this register has no public drawdown figure for it. The recorded USAspending search found no entry for EDA award ED25HDQ0G0009. The disbursement row in group B stays empty because no single figure spans the register, not because nothing has been paid. It is still the row a board should ask about first, because a fully assigned award that has disbursed nothing and a fully assigned award that has disbursed everything look identical on this page.

Group C · talent

Polymer credentials fell by more than half in 2021 and have come back only a little

Federal education data covers the degree side of the talent question: how many people finish a polymer credential each year at the two universities in this file that confer them, Case Western Reserve and the University of Akron. Kent State is carried alongside them for the adjacent materials degrees and confers no polymer credential here. The completions query names three institutions rather than asking the region, so the universities counted are the universities in the file. It says nothing about PIC’s own workforce programs, which are the two empty rows in group C. Materials credentials at Case Western and Kent State ran 29, 21 and 33 across the same three years, 2021 to 2023, ending higher than they started while polymer credentials stayed near their low.

Polymer credentials ran between 118 and 179 a year through 2020, then dropped to 54

Credentials at every level, at those same three universities, in the federal Classification of Instructional Programs (CIP) codes the occupations page classes as polymer, by federal reporting year. Higher is more graduates. The 2020 to 2022 counts are read from the NCES completions files: the data mirror this series is otherwise built from filed those years a year late and served one of them under no year at all. Three readings come off this series: 54 is the 2021 low, 63 is the latest year, and the 60 in the scorecard row is the average of 2021, 2022 and 2023.

The objection

Why a blank cell is worth more than a close-enough one

The obvious complaint about this page is that seven blanks make a poor board document, and that a rounded band or a rough share would at least give the room something to talk about.

The answer is that a scorecard is read as a measurement, and a number in a scorecard cell is treated as one for years after whoever typed it has forgotten where it came from. Once a plausible membership figure sits in this table, nobody re-derives it; they compare against it. The seven blanks are therefore doing the work: each one names the register that holds the real number, the definition that number has to satisfy, and the cadence it has to arrive on, so that populating it later is a lookup rather than a judgment call. The cost of the blank is one awkward board meeting. The cost of a plausible wrong number is a year of decisions taken against it.

Public records answer where PIC’s award money was assigned and say nothing about whether PIC can pay for itself.

Eight of the fifteen accountable rows compute from PIC’s published award register and federal and state records: $79.2 million of $85.3 million awarded carries a named recipient, and 63 polymer credentials were awarded at the institutions in this file in 2023 against a three-year average of 60. Seven rows, including all four on membership and revenue and the one on disbursement, are defined and empty. The disbursement row is empty because no public figure spans the register, not because nothing has been paid: the federal ledger publishes an outlay on seven of the eight federal lines, and the state grant has no drawdown figure in this register. Three more are cluster context that belongs to the region rather than to PIC, and they stay in their own group for that reason.
