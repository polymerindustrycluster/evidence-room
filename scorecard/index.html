PIC’s own scorecard · measurement coverage, August 2026

Public records fill eight of the fifteen rows PIC answers for, and none of the four on revenue.

The award register shows $79.2 million of the $85.3 million PIC has won sitting on an executed line with a named recipient. It records no payment against any of it, and the four rows that would tell a board whether PIC can pay for itself are kept in registers a public repository may not carry.

By John Swanson, Polymer Industry Cluster desk · Analysis and graphics by Claude (Anthropic) · Data from the PIC award register, IPEDS and BLS · August 2026

Read this first

This is an internal working view, built only from public federal and state data. It is not a published account of PIC’s finances or membership.

This repository is public and its history is permanent, so it carries no member, applicant or personal data. Seven rows below are therefore unpopulated on purpose: their measurement lives in PIC’s membership register, general ledger and drawdown records. Each one is published here with its definition, cadence and target field ready, and with a visible empty state instead of a figure. Filling them needs a copy of this page kept outside a public repository, and nothing on this page should be read as an estimate of what those cells would say.

The scorecard

Seven of the fifteen rows PIC answers for are blank

One row per metric, in the four groups a board reads in order. Groups A, B and C are what PIC is accountable for. Group D is the regional economy PIC works on and does not control, kept at the bottom, set apart, and never averaged into anything above it.

Eight rows compute from public records, seven wait on a private copy, three are context

Status is marked twice: by the rule down the left edge and by the current reading itself. A blank cell reads “not published here” and never reads zero.

What the target and owner columns mean. No target here was set by PIC. A target showing a figure is a ceiling fixed by a signed award document; every other target reads “not set”, and that absence is the finding rather than a gap in the build. No owner cell names a person, because this repository holds no owner assignment for any row, and an EOS row without a named owner is not yet a scorecard row.

The distinction the groups exist to hold: cluster health measures the regional polymer economy, which PIC influences through award money, convening and programs but does not control, while the scorecard measures what PIC itself is accountable for. They must never be averaged or scored on one line. Letting organisational growth stand in for regional outcomes, or regional growth stand in for organisational performance, is the standard way economic-development reporting goes wrong: the cluster adds jobs for reasons no association caused, and the association reports them as delivery.

Group B · award delivery

Every award dollar without a named recipient sits inside one state grant

The register carries three public awards. Two of them are fully assigned: all seven EDA implementation awards are obligated straight to their project leads, and the APEX workforce award is committed in one line. The unassigned balance is entirely Ohio money, held in two workstreams whose sub-grants are still being executed.

$6.2M of the $85.3M awarded has no named recipient yet, and all of it is Ohio money

One bar per public award, drawn to the same dollar scale. The solid part is money on an executed line naming the organisation that holds it; the hatched part is money awarded to PIC with the recipient not yet named.

One quantity is missing from that chart and cannot be drawn from any public source: how much of the money has actually been paid. An obligation is a legal commitment to pay, and an executed sub-grant is a signed agreement to pay. Neither is a payment. The drawdown row in group B is empty for that reason, and it is the row a board should ask about first, because a fully assigned award that has disbursed nothing and a fully assigned award that has disbursed everything look identical on this page.

Group C · talent

Polymer credentials fell by more than half in 2022 and have not come back

Federal education data covers the degree side of the talent question: how many people finish a polymer credential at the three regional institutions each year. It says nothing about PIC’s own workforce programs, which are the two empty rows in group C. Materials credentials at the same institutions moved the other way over the same three years, rising from 20 to 33.

Polymer credentials ran near 120 a year for a decade, then dropped to 54

Credentials at every level, all polymer CIP codes at the three regional institutions, by federal reporting year. Higher is more graduates. The two pale columns are years this page does not use.

The objection

Why a blank cell is worth more than a close-enough one

The obvious complaint about this page is that seven blanks make a poor board document, and that a rounded band or a rough share would at least give the room something to talk about.

The answer is that a scorecard is read as a measurement, and a number in a scorecard cell is treated as one for years after whoever typed it has forgotten where it came from. Once a plausible membership figure sits in this table, nobody re-derives it; they compare against it. The seven blanks are therefore doing the work: each one names the register that holds the real number, the definition that number has to satisfy, and the cadence it has to arrive on, so that populating it later is a lookup rather than a judgment call. The cost of the blank is one awkward board meeting. The cost of a plausible wrong number is a year of decisions taken against it.

Public records answer where PIC’s award money was assigned and say nothing about whether PIC can pay for itself.

Eight of the fifteen accountable rows compute from federal and state records: $79.2 million of $85.3 million awarded carries a named recipient, and 63 polymer credentials were awarded in the region in 2023 against a three-year average of 80. Seven rows, including all four on revenue and the one on disbursement, are defined and empty. Three more are cluster context that belongs to the region rather than to PIC, and they stay in their own group for that reason.
