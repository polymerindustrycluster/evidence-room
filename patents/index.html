Polymer patent applications, by inventor address · 2015–2023

American polymer patenting fell by a fifth. Ohio fell with it.

The state whose rubber city named the industry filed 342 polymer patent applications in 2023, down a quarter from 2015 and roughly in step with its industry: filings with an American inventor fell 22 percent nationwide while American patenting across all technologies barely moved. Ohio kept about a tenth of the shrinking field. What is Ohio’s own is quieter and separate: its patenting across all technologies fell 12.5 percent against the nation’s 1.6, polymer and everything else alike.

By John Swanson, Polymer Industry Cluster desk · Analysis and graphics by Claude (Anthropic) · Data USPTO Open Data Portal, applications by filing year · August 2026

Compared with what, twice

The fall is the industry’s, and Ohio’s slide is its own second story

One series proves nothing by falling, so here are four on one scale. American polymer filings ended 2023 at 78.2 on a 2015 base of 100 while American filings across all technologies ended at 98.4: a polymer-specific collapse, nationwide. Ohio’s polymer line, at 75.2, tracks its industry a few points below it. And Ohio’s all-technology line, at 87.5 against the nation’s 98.4, is a separate, milder Ohio-wide decline that has nothing particular to do with polymer.

Four lines, two stories

Patent applications with a US inventor address, indexed to 2015 = 100, complete filing years only. Polymer (CPC C08) against all technology classes, for Ohio and for the country.

USPTO Open Data Portal, patent applications by filing year, CPC C08 (organic macromolecular compounds) by inventor correspondence address, national series filtered to US inventor addresses, fetched 2026-08-30. C08 is one defensible definition of polymer, and a choice.

The count with no address filter is a trap, and it nearly caught this page. Ask the same endpoint for C08 filings without naming a country and the series ends at 99: essentially flat. That number is every filing at the US Patent Office from anywhere on earth, and it holds level only because foreign-origin filings filled the hole American ones left. A replication reviewer, rebuilding this page’s numbers from the rendered text alone, reproduced the 99 and could not reproduce the label on it, which is how the error surfaced before publication. Filter the inventor country or say you did not.

The level, and the trap in the recent years

The last two bars always look like a collapse, and never are

A patent application publishes about eighteen months after it is filed, so the newest filing years are undercounted by construction: read on 30 August 2026, the 2025 bar holds 266 and the 2026 bar holds 20. Neither is a finding about Ohio. The solid bars are complete years and carry the real level; the hollow bars are drawn so the shape of the lag is visible instead of taken on trust.

Where in the state, held loosely

The metro counts are too small to rank, and are printed anyway

City-level counts run on tens of filings, where one corporate lab moving or one attorney changing the address on a docket moves the line. Across the complete years Akron’s count ranges from 46 to 87 with no trend a reader should lean on, and Youngstown’s touches zero. They are here because a regional page owes the reader its own region’s numbers, not because they can carry a claim.

Inventor correspondence address, city field, complete years only. A city is not a metro area: these are the four anchor cities by name, and suburban addresses fall outside them.

The pipeline echoes it. The talent page found the region’s polymer programs conferred 63 degrees in 2023 against an average of 139 a year through 2021. Filings and degrees are different instruments on different scopes, one statewide and one regional, and they are falling together: the inputs to invention and its recorded outputs, both roughly halved or headed there inside a decade.

The trap this measure carries

An address is not a laboratory

Every count on this page keys on the inventor’s correspondence address. Key on the applicant’s instead, the company rather than the person, and Ohio’s all-time polymer count drops from 8,854 to 3,948. Neither is wrong. They answer different questions: where inventors live against who owns what they invent.

The first record this page’s pull ever returned makes the point by itself: a tire-technology application assigned to The Goodyear Tire & Rubber Company of Akron, Ohio, with the inventor resident in Colmar-Berg, Luxembourg. Counted by applicant, that is an Akron invention. Counted by inventor, it is a Luxembourg one. This page counts inventors, because its question is whether invention still happens here rather than who holds the title, and a reader who wants the other answer now knows both numbers.

For a replicator, the exact leaf fields, because guessing them is the stall: the year filter is applicationMetaData.filingDate:[2015-01-01 TO 2015-12-31], the state is geographicRegionCode and the country countryCode on applicationMetaData.inventorBag.correspondenceAddressBag (swap inventorBag for applicantBag for the other basis), the city is cityName on the same bag, and the all-technology series is the same query with the CPC clause removed.

The field is shrinking at home, and Ohio is shrinking with it.

American polymer filings fell 22 percent over the eight complete years while American patenting overall fell 2; Ohio’s polymer filings fell 25 and its share of the shrinking American field held near one in ten. The number to watch is not Ohio against the country. It is polymer against everything else.
