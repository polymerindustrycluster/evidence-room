Polymer patent applications, by inventor address · 2015–2023

American polymer patenting fell by a fifth. Ohio fell with it.

Ohio, home of Akron and the American rubber industry, filed 342 polymer patent applications in 2023, down a quarter from 2015. American polymer filings fell almost as far: applications with an American inventor were down 22 percent nationwide, while American patenting across all technologies barely moved. Ohio kept about a tenth of those shrinking American polymer filings. Ohio’s own decline is a quieter, separate story. Its patenting across all technologies fell 12.5 percent against the nation’s 1.6 percent, and that milder slide runs through polymer and everything else alike.

By John Swanson, Polymer Industry Cluster desk · Analysis and graphics by Claude (Anthropic) · Data USPTO Open Data Portal, applications by filing year · August 2026

Compared with what, twice

The fall is the industry’s, and Ohio’s slide is its own second story

One series proves nothing by falling, so here are four on one scale. American polymer filings ended 2023 at 78.2 on a 2015 base of 100 while American filings across all technologies ended at 98.4: a polymer-specific collapse, nationwide. Ohio’s polymer line, at 75.2, tracks American polymer filings a few points below them. And Ohio’s all-technology line, at 87.5 against the nation’s 98.4, is a separate, milder Ohio-wide decline that has nothing particular to do with polymer.

Four lines, two stories

Patent applications with a US inventor address, indexed to 2015 = 100, and only complete filing years: years old enough that everything filed in them has already published. Polymer means class C08 of the Cooperative Patent Classification (CPC), the international system that files every patent by subject, set against all technology classes, for Ohio and for the country.

USPTO Open Data Portal, patent applications by filing year, CPC C08 (organic macromolecular compounds) by inventor correspondence address, national series filtered to US inventor addresses, fetched 2026-08-30. C08 is one defensible definition of polymer, and a choice.

The count with no address filter is a trap, and it nearly caught this page. Run the same request for C08 filings without naming a country and the series ends at 99: essentially flat. That number is every filing at the US Patent Office from anywhere on earth, and it holds level only because foreign-origin filings filled the hole American ones left. Before publication, a reviewer rebuilt this page’s numbers from the printed text alone. The 99 came back the same. The sentence attached to it did not: the wording never said the count was worldwide. That is how the error was caught. Filter the inventor country or say you did not.

The level, and the trap in the recent years

The last two bars always look like a collapse, and never are

A patent application publishes about eighteen months after it is filed, so the newest filing years are undercounted by construction. Read on 30 August 2026, Ohio’s polymer count for 2025 stood at 266 and its count for 2026 at 20. Neither is a finding about Ohio: both years are still filling. Only the complete filing years carry the real level.

Ohio polymer applications by filing year

Solid bars are complete filing years. Hollow bars are years still filling through the publication lag, drawn at their current count.

Same USPTO request as the chart above. Ohio’s polymer filings peaked in 2017 at 498, so measuring from that peak to 2023 would make the fall look steeper than measuring from 2015. This page indexes from 2015, the first year fetched, rather than from the year that would make the decline look worst.

Where in the state, held loosely

The metro counts are too small to rank, and are printed anyway

City-level counts run on tens of filings, where one corporate lab moving or one attorney changing the address on a docket moves the line. Across the complete years Akron’s count ranges from 46 to 87 with no trend a reader should lean on, and Youngstown’s touches zero. They are here because a regional page owes the reader its own region’s numbers, not because they can carry a claim.

Inventor correspondence address, city field, complete years only. A city is not a metro area: these four are the cities themselves, Akron, Cleveland, Canton and Youngstown, and suburban addresses fall outside them.

The pipeline echoes it. The talent page found the region’s polymer programs conferred 63 degrees in 2023 against an average of 142 a year through 2021. The two counts cover different ground: filings are statewide, degrees are regional. They are falling together anyway. The inputs to invention and its recorded outputs have both roughly halved, or are headed there, inside a decade.

The trap this measure carries

An address is not a laboratory

Every count on this page keys on the inventor’s correspondence address. Key on the applicant’s instead, the company rather than the person, and Ohio’s all-time polymer count drops from 8,854 to 3,948. All-time here carries no year filter at all: it is every filing year the Patent Office holds, not the 2015 to 2023 window charted above. Neither is wrong. They answer different questions: where inventors live against who owns what they invent.

The first record this page’s request ever returned makes the point by itself: a tire-technology application assigned to The Goodyear Tire & Rubber Company of Akron, Ohio, with the inventor resident in Colmar-Berg, Luxembourg. Counted by applicant, that is an Akron invention. Counted by inventor, it is a Luxembourg one. That single record runs the opposite way from the two totals above, and both hold: switching to the applicant basis adds filings like this one, where the company is in Ohio and the inventor is not, and removes many more where the inventor is in Ohio and the company is not. This page counts inventors, because its question is whether invention still happens here rather than who holds the title, and a reader who wants the other answer now knows both numbers.

The rest of this paragraph is for a replicator rebuilding these counts from the source. Guessing the exact leaf field names is what stops most attempts, so here they are. The year filter is applicationMetaData.filingDate:[2015-01-01 TO 2015-12-31], the state is geographicRegionCode and the country countryCode on applicationMetaData.inventorBag.correspondenceAddressBag (swap inventorBag for applicantBag for the other basis), the city is cityName on the same bag, and the all-technology series is the same query with the CPC clause removed.

The polymer field is shrinking at home, and Ohio is shrinking with it.

American polymer filings fell 22 percent over the eight complete years, while American patenting overall fell under 2. Ohio’s polymer filings fell 25 percent, and its share of those shrinking American polymer filings held near one in ten. The number to watch is not Ohio against the country. It is polymer against everything else.
