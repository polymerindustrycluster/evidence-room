Location quotient, computed from public data

PIC quotes a concentration figure it licenses. Here is one it owns.

Team NEO's 2.69× is a Lightcast product: one number, one year, no decomposition, and nobody outside the license can check it. The Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes the components free. This is eleven years of concentration for six polymer industries across twelve counties — reproduced to within 0.005 of the figure the bureau publishes itself.

Location quotient (LQ) — how concentrated an industry is here compared with the country as a whole. An LQ of 1.0 means the region has exactly its national share of those jobs; 2.69 means nearly three times that share. An establishment is a single physical site, not a company: one firm with three plants is three establishments.

The honest caveat

The highest concentrations sit on the smallest bases

A location quotient is a ratio, so a county with six plants can post a bigger number than a county with sixty. Plotted against the jobs behind it, the dramatic figures move to the left where they belong.
