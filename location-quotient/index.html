Which polymer industry is this region most concentrated in? · BLS QCEW, 2015–2025

The region’s strongest polymer concentration is paint, at 5.96 times the national share.

Rubber products, the industry Akron is named for, run 2.44×. Paint and coatings have led all six polymer industries in every year since 2015, and Cuyahoga County holds three of every four disclosed paint jobs in the region. Every figure here is computed from free Bureau of Labor Statistics files and reproduces the bureau’s own published location quotient to the last digit it prints, so anyone who disputes the ranking can re-run it.

Location quotient (LQ): how concentrated an industry is here compared with the country as a whole. An LQ of 1.0 means the region has exactly its national share of those jobs; 5.96 means nearly six times that share. An establishment is a single physical site, not a company: one firm with three plants counts as three establishments.

The finding

Paint leads all six polymer industries, and has every year since 2015

A location quotient of 1.0 means the region has exactly its national share of an industry. Paint and coatings have not been anywhere near 1.0 since this series starts. The line sits above 5.4× for eleven straight years and runs more than double whichever industry is second. Ohio as a whole is already concentrated in paint at 3.78×, and these twelve counties are well above the state.

Who is in that cell. QCEW cannot name an employer, so the grid shows a concentration and not a company. Two public facts sit behind it: the Sherwin-Williams Company is headquartered in Cleveland, in Cuyahoga County, and RPM International is headquartered in Medina, which posts the region’s fifth paint reading at 2.9×. QCEW codes an establishment by what it does, so a corporate campus can land in the same industry as a plant.
