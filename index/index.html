What the geography labels, job counts and claim counts mean

The teal label names the geography used in that story. PIC-12 means Ashtabula, Cuyahoga, Geauga, Lake, Lorain, Mahoning, Medina, Portage, Stark, Summit, Trumbull and Wayne counties. PIC-12+2 adds Columbiana and Tuscarawas to those twelve; only the supply-chain register uses it. Other stories use metro, state or national records. Claim counts describe checks against the underlying files; manual claims depend on a cited document.

Why the job counts differ. Stories count different industries, years and surveys, so their totals for the twelve counties are not meant to match. 17,770 is plastics and rubber products (NAICS 326) in 2025, from the payroll census (cluster health). 18,594 is the same industry a year earlier (rankings). 17,725 is the same industry counted by a different Census survey at the start of 2025’s third quarter (churn). 24,032 adds resin and paint (3252 and 3255), 2025, a floor because some counties are withheld (concentration). 33,529 is all chemical manufacturing plus plastics and rubber (325 and 326), 2025, counted once (wages). Concentration differs the same way: plastics and rubber fill 2.32 times the national share of jobs across the twelve counties in 2025 and 4.69 times in the Akron metro alone in 2024.

Where does Ohio rank among states, and Akron among metros?

First in the country

Ohio holds 54,846 plastics and rubber jobs, 10,248 more than second-place Texas, with all 50 states and Puerto Rico disclosed; DC is absent. Akron is 6th of the 155 metros that disclose, where 1st is the largest; 227 metros are withheld and are simply unknown.

Where do the people who work in a county live?

Summit County’s own residents hold 48.4 percent of its 265,460 jobs. Ashtabula is the most self-contained of the twelve at 68 percent. Every county draws some workers from outside its boundary; the balance varies by county. These are jobs across all industries, not polymer jobs alone.

How does chemical, plastics and rubber pay compare with other factory work?

Across 51 county-industry pairings, median group-average pay is 0.99 times county manufacturing pay, from 1.16 times for chemicals and 0.87 times for plastics and rubber, and 1.21 times county all-jobs pay. These are group averages, not individual workers’ wages. The comparisons describe pay; they do not establish a causal industry premium.

How big is the Tech Hub award next to the federal money that arrives every year?

Federal contract obligations for work performed in the twelve counties, under chemical, plastics and rubber manufacturing codes, averaged $41.4 million a year in 2025 dollars across fiscal 2019–2025. Annual totals ranged from $20.5 million to $59.9 million. The one-time $51.0 million Tech Hub award is about 1.2 years of that average, a rough size and not like for like: a competitive grant and routine purchase orders are different kinds of money. Fiscal 2026 is incomplete and excluded; 2019 and 2021 each exceeded the award. A different measure: the prime contracts under those codes with obligations in fiscal 2019–2026 carry $329.5 million counted over each award’s whole life, and two Department of Defense suppliers, the survival-equipment maker RFD Beaufort and Goodyear, hold 56 percent of it.

National position

Are jobs and workplaces moving together?

Private plastics and rubber jobs fell from 19,811 in 2022 to 17,770 in 2025, down 10.3 percent, while establishments moved from 361 to 365. These are annual averages across the twelve counties, not a panel of the same factories. The article compares Ohio and US changes before considering what is distinctive.

Which polymer industry is this region most concentrated in?

Paint and coatings fill 5.96 times as large a share of local jobs as they do of the country’s, and have led the three cluster industries every year since 2015. Rubber products alone, NAICS 3262, the narrow industry Akron is named for, run 2.44 times; the wider plastics and rubber family that contains it, NAICS 326, runs 2.32 times. Concentration is what makes a region distinctive and what leaves it exposed.

Does the region still invent the thing it is named for?

Ohio inventor addresses account for 342 polymer patent applications in 2023, down 25 percent from 2015. The comparable US-inventor count fell 22 percent. The article separates this national decline from Ohio’s wider patent trend and explains the publication lag and address limits.

Workforce

How much hiring and separating sits behind a headcount that fell?

111,529 hires and 111,361 separations across 55 quarters produced a net flow of 168 jobs. In the average quarter hires run 10.8 percent of the workforce and separations 10.7 percent. The headcount, a separate Census estimate that need not agree with the flows, fell 719 over the same span.

Where did the public money go?

$106.3 million in all: $85.3 million across three public awards, plus $21.0 million of matching money that came with them. The awards and their match are drawn to scale; the threads into the twenty-one named recipients are not. Operating conditions

What do these wages buy once local prices are counted?

Among 29 matched metros with at least 2,000 plastics and rubber jobs in 2024, Akron ranks 13th on industry-average weekly pay and 8th after adjusting for local price levels. It is above the sample midpoint on both measures. Cleveland also clears that threshold. Among all 155 disclosed matched metros, Youngstown–Warren ranks 45th after adjustment, ahead of Akron at 48th. Its 960 jobs exclude it from the headline ranking solely because of the 2,000-job threshold. Canton–Massillon’s wage data are source-suppressed. These are metro readings on matched 2024 boundaries.

What happened to the polymer technician pipeline?

Among records with more than ten completions in 1991–2023, 20 of 73 technician records (27 percent) reported a completion in 2023, against 48 percent of degree records and selected peer-trade records. Reporting under these codes does not establish whether teaching closed, changed labels or moved format.

How much of the 2022 price spike came back out of finished polymer prices?

Finished prices remain elevated, before inflation

Natural gas gave back its whole 2022 spike, crude about half, resin about a third, and finished polymer products none of it. The further a price sits from the wellhead, the less of the spike came back out.

How much do these figures change after publication?

Almost every number moves

259 of 273 published months in three producer-price series, the prices plants charge for chemicals and allied products and for plastic and rubber goods, were changed later by a typical 0.15 percent. That the change is small is the finding, and the scope is those three series only.

A failure means a published sentence no longer matches its source. Fix the sentence or the computation, never the check.

Rebuild the data from origin

Start with the source-by-source replication guide. Acquisition scripts have different inputs and availability limits; there is no single wildcard command that rebuilds every source. The company register behind chain is not public, and some timeline rows come from PIC project records.

Re-running all of them is one command in the repository, python _data/build/verify_claims.py, and the listing doubles as the brief for a hostile reviewer.

The federal sources behind these pages, named once so a reader can find them. QCEW is the Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages, the payroll count behind every jobs and wage figure here. IPEDS is the Integrated Postsecondary Education Data System, the federal record of every degree conferred. NAICS is the North American Industry Classification System, the codes that decide which employers count as polymer. BEA is the Bureau of Economic Analysis, which publishes regional price levels, and BLS is the Bureau of Labor Statistics.

If a sentence here is wrong, the check stored beside it is the fastest way to show it.

Each check names, in advance, the finding that would break the sentence it guards, and ties that sentence to the data file it came from rather than to the world. Where that was not enough, the correction is dated in a log that is only ever appended to.
