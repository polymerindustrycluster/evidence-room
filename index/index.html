How to check this work

1. Re-run every claim

cd _data/build
python verify_claims.py

Every published sentence is an assertion against the data that produced it. A failure means the sentence no longer matches its source — fix the sentence or the computation, never the assertion.

2. Read the falsification conditions

python verify_claims.py --list

Each claim carries what would make it wrong. This output is the brief for a hostile reviewer — it is the attack surface, written down by the person who made the claims.

3. Attack the manual claims first

Claims quoted from documents rather than datasets cannot be machine-checked. They print as MANL and are the most likely place a wrong reading survives. They need a human or a council lane.

4. Rebuild the data from origin

python fetch_*.py && python derive_*.py

Every dataset is reproducible from _data/build/ with free public APIs. Nothing depends on a private table, and nothing writes to production.

Two footprints, three registers, and a suppression rule. Read those before quoting anything.

Federal-data pages use PIC-12, matching the cluster-health dashboard. Vault-sourced pages use NEO-14, which is how company records are tagged. They share ten counties and never reconcile. PIC's measurement register is 3252 + 3255 + 326 — NAICS 325 is context, not cluster. A withheld cell is never a zero. All three rules are in _data/METHODS-SOP.md, and the footprints themselves are defined once in _data/build/footprints.py — the file every page imports them from, so a rule stated here cannot disagree with the code.

The federal sources behind these pages, named once so a reader can find them: QCEW — the Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages, the payroll count behind every jobs and wage figure here. IPEDS — the Integrated Postsecondary Education Data System, the federal record of every degree conferred. NAICS — the North American Industry Classification System, the codes that decide which employers count as polymer. BEA is the Bureau of Economic Analysis, which publishes regional price levels; BLS is the Bureau of Labor Statistics. TRL — technology readiness level, a 1-to-9 scale from idea to production.
