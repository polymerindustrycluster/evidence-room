54,846 of those jobs, ten thousand more than second-place Texas, and every state disclosed in the Bureau of Labor Statistics count. Underneath the ranking sit the questions a member firm actually argues about: what the work pays, and who is available to do it.

Before anything leaves the building

Every sentence here is a test that can fail

Each published sentence is stored as an assertion against the data that produced it, next to the condition that would prove it wrong. Re-running all of them is one command in the repository, and the listing doubles as the brief for a hostile reviewer.

The commands, for anyone who wants to run them

Re-run every claim, or read what would break it

cd _data/build
python verify_claims.py
python verify_claims.py --list

A failure means a published sentence no longer matches its source. Fix the sentence or the computation, never the assertion.

Rebuild the data from origin

python fetch_*.py && python derive_*.py

Every dataset is reproducible from _data/build/ with free public APIs. Nothing reads a private table, and nothing writes to production.

The federal sources behind these pages, named once so a reader can find them. QCEW is the Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages, the payroll count behind every jobs and wage figure here. IPEDS is the Integrated Postsecondary Education Data System, the federal record of every degree conferred. NAICS is the North American Industry Classification System, the codes that decide which employers count as polymer. BEA is the Bureau of Economic Analysis, which publishes regional price levels, and BLS is the Bureau of Labor Statistics.

The ranking opens the meeting. The twelve pages here are what has to hold up in it.
