Where can you learn polymer science?

147 institution records since 1991; 41 of them recorded a polymer award in 2023.

Three of the 147 account for more than a third of all the degrees and certificates in that record: the University of Massachusetts Lowell, Ferris State University in Big Rapids, Michigan, and the University of Akron. Each record follows a federal institution identifier; one school can have more than one. Teaching reported outside polymer codes is missing. The 41 institutions are one year of filings, not a roll-call of open schools: an institution absent from it may still be teaching the subject, under another code or none.

By John Swanson, Polymer Industry Cluster desk · Analysis and graphics by Claude (Anthropic), updated with Codex (OpenAI) · Data IPEDS completions by 6-digit CIP, IPEDS institutional directory

Words on this page
  • IPEDS, the Integrated Postsecondary Education Data System, is the federal record of the degrees and certificates each college reports conferring.
  • CIP, the Classification of Instructional Programs, is the federal code a college files each program under; teaching filed under another code is missing from a count by code.
  • A completion is one degree or certificate a college reports conferring.
  • A unitid is the federal identifier IPEDS gives each institution record; one school can hold more than one.

The record is a floor, not a census, and the map says so on its own face.

If you run a plant here

  • Your nearest deep polymer program is in the region: the University of Akron is one of the three institution records that together hold more than a third of every polymer degree and certificate in the federal record.
  • Before you write off a college as a source of polymer training, check with it directly: a pale mark here means only that it filed no completion under the three polymer CIP codes in 2023.
