Polymer wages against the towns they sit in

In 40 of 51 measurable cells, a polymer job pays more than the county average.

PIC's workforce case rests on the claim that these are good jobs. The claim is checkable: the Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes average weekly wage by county and industry every year. Be clear about the bar. The county all-industry average includes retail and hospitality, so beating it is a low hurdle for manufacturing — it is the right control for local cost of living, and a weak test of “good”. These are also means, not medians, computed per disclosed cell, so one large employer moves a county and every cell counts the same regardless of size.

The chart

Every county and industry against its own local average

A value of 1.0 means the polymer job pays exactly what the average job in that county pays. To the right is a premium; to the left, a discount. The comparison is deliberately local — a wage that beats the national average but trails its own town is not a good job in that town.
