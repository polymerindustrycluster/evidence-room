The five measures

Each one, the baseline it is read against, and what it cannot see

PIC has set no target for any of these five, so no tile reports distance from one. Each reports a level, a direction against a stated baseline, and the page it came from, where the working is shown in full.

How to read a move

How far these numbers normally move, and what nobody has measured

The band behind each bar is built one way for all five measures. Take the measure’s own published history, work out the change between every consecutive pair of years, hold out the newest one, and compare it against the middle and the largest of the rest. A move at or under the middle is a year this measure has had many times.

That band describes how far a series travels between published years. It is not a revision band, and the difference decides how much weight a fresh figure can carry. A revision band would say how far the year just published will move once the agencies restate it, and this repository has measured that for exactly three producer-price indexes: 259 reference months of archived vintages, a middle revision of 0.15 percent of the level and a largest of 1.42 percent. For the employment, degree and spending series on this page, nobody has measured it. The revisions page says so itself and names archived employment vintages as the file it needs.

Withheld is not zero. Three of the five measures are built from county cells, and the Bureau of Labor Statistics withholds a cell when publishing it would identify an employer. The set it withholds changes from year to year, so a raw comparison of two annual totals mixes real change with the bureau’s publication decisions. Where that applies, the direction on this page is read from the cells present in every year.
