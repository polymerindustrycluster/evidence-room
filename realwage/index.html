Wages against local prices, 2024

Local prices move Akron from 13th to 8th among 29 disclosed polymer metros.

Akron’s average plastics-and-rubber paycheck is $1,291 a week. Adjusted for local prices, it buys like $1,383 at national-average prices. The comparison covers metros with at least 2,000 private jobs in plastics and rubber products manufacturing (NAICS 326). Akron is above the middle on both measures. Across all 155 matched, disclosed metros, Akron moves from 60th to 48th. The many metros whose wages are withheld remain unranked.

By John Swanson, Polymer Industry Cluster desk · Analysis and graphics by Claude (Anthropic), updated with Codex (OpenAI) · Data: BLS Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages and BEA Regional Price Parities, 2024 · September 2026

A corrected comparison

The geography must match before the wages can be compared

Both sources here use the metropolitan boundaries in Office of Management and Budget (OMB) bulletin 23-01, issued July 21, 2023. BLS began using those boundaries in 2024; BEA’s downloaded price table explicitly declares the same definition. Matching a metro code and a year alone was not enough in the earlier edition.

Correction, September 8, 2026. The old 2023 comparison ranked Akron 33rd on wages and 19th on purchasing power among 56 metros, but joined different boundary vintages. We withdraw that ranking and its below-to-above-median claim. Cleveland’s old 2023 wage was disclosed ($1,149 on 7,550 jobs); its absence was a failed geography join, not suppression. The rebuilt 2024 sample includes Cleveland. Canton, Chicago and New York are explicitly withheld in the 2024 source. The change in sample and ranks between editions is not a time trend.

Nominal is the average weekly wage in dollars. Price-adjusted divides that wage by the local price index / 100. BEA’s index compares consumption prices with the national level of 100; Akron’s 93.4 is about 7 percent lower. It is not a household budget. Industry averages do not compare the same occupation or hours worked across places.

Youngstown–Warren has 960 industry jobs, below this chart’s 2,000-job cutoff. Across all 155 matched metros, its adjusted pay ranks 45th, above Akron’s 48th. The display cutoff selects larger industry workforces, not higher wages.

The price comparison

Akron is below the sample’s middle price level

Among all 155 metros with disclosed wages and matched prices, 61 have lower prices than Akron. The middle metro’s price index is 94.8, compared with Akron’s 93.4. This is the middle of a disclosed industry sample, not a ranking of every US metro. Each metro counts once in the median.

Wage against price

A larger paycheck can still buy more

Los Angeles averages $1,908 a week and buys like $1,680 after adjustment, above Akron’s $1,383. Its higher prices reduce its wage advantage; they do not erase it. Five metros, including Seattle and Phoenix, pay more than Akron before adjustment and less afterward. A real offer can differ from either industry average.
