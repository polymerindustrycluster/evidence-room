Polymer wages against local price levels, 2023

The same paycheck ranks below average in dollars and above average in what it buys.

For a member trying to hire against Chicago or Boston money, the number on the offer is only half the argument: the same paycheck stretches about 10 percent further in Akron than in Chicago. Every wage comparison PIC has ever published is nominal, which quietly assumes a dollar is a dollar everywhere. The Bureau of Economic Analysis prices a fixed national basket in every metro (US average = 100), so the assumption is testable. Among the 56 metros with a real polymer workforce, Akron is 33rd on nominal weekly wages and 19th on what those wages actually buy. The climb crosses the median, and it is smaller than the pitch PIC usually makes. This page says why.

Nominal — the number printed on the paycheck, before any adjustment for what things cost where you live. Its opposite here is “real,” meaning what the paycheck actually buys. The median is the middle value, with as many above as below — not the average, which a few very high earners can pull upward.

What it means in an offer letter

Against Chicago, the price advantage is worth about $6,600 a year

Akron’s average polymer paycheck is $1,238 a week, about $64,000 a year. To hand a candidate the same buying power, a Chicago employer has to write about $71,000, and the spread widens up the price ladder: matching it in San Francisco takes about $81,000. These are averages of covered paychecks in each metro, not any one person’s offer; the point is the size of the wedge a recruiter can put in a candidate’s spreadsheet.

A Chicago offer has to clear $71,000 to beat Akron’s $64,000. Against New York, Los Angeles or Seattle, the bigger paycheck buys less outright.
