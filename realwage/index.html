Polymer wages against local price levels, 2023

The same paycheck ranks below average in dollars and above average in what it buys.

Every wage comparison PIC has ever published is nominal, which quietly assumes a dollar is a dollar everywhere. the Bureau of Economic Analysis publishes a price index for each metro — a fixed national basket, US average 100 — so the assumption is testable. Among the 56 metros with a real polymer workforce, Akron is 33rd on nominal weekly wages and 19th on what those wages actually buy. The adjustment does not just help; it crosses the median. It is also smaller than the pitch PIC usually makes, and this page says why.

Nominal — the number printed on the paycheck, before any adjustment for what things cost where you live. Its opposite here is “real,” meaning what the paycheck actually buys. The median is the middle value, with as many above as below — not the average, which a few very high earners can pull upward.

Wage against price

The two things a recruiter is actually trading off

Nominal wage across, local price level up. The diagonal is constant purchasing power — every metro on the same line offers the same real wage. Up and to the left is a worse deal than the salary implies; down and to the right is a better one.

Recruiting against Chicago on salary is a losing argument. Recruiting on what the salary buys is a winning one, by about fourteen places.
