Public awards to the polymer cluster, as of August 2026

Three public awards, $85.3 million, and no two move the money the same way.

That is what governments awarded to the Greater Akron polymer cluster. Partners and the state promised $21.0 million more beside it, and the two added together are the $106.3 million the region reports as secured. Of the money actually awarded, $79.2 million already names who receives it. This is every dollar, every program, and every organization it reaches.

By John Swanson, Polymer Industry Cluster desk · Analysis and graphics by Claude (Anthropic) · Data signed federal Notices of Award and the executed Ohio grant agreement · Figures as of 13 August 2026

Three machines

Each award commits its money by a different route

Meet the awards one at a time; the combined map follows. Each award is a machine here, meaning a route by which the money travels from government to the organizations that spend it. Each keeps its color on every figure below. Two short names run through all three: the Polymer Industry Cluster (PIC), the body that publishes this page, and the federal Economic Development Administration (EDA), the agency behind two of the three awards.

  1. Machine 1 · EDA Sustainable Polymers Tech Hub · $51.00M federal

    The Tech Hub award is split into seven implementation awards, one for each project lead. EDA obligates each of them, meaning it commits the money straight to that lead, so no federal dollar passes through PIC’s hands.

    Partners promised $7.16M alongside it. Largest check: BioVerde, $11.12M, for bio-based butadiene, the building block of synthetic rubber. Trace it on the map

  2. Machine 2 · Ohio Innovation Hub · $31.25M state

    The state’s $31.25M grant lands in one Akron hub and splits five ways. The biggest slice, $18.52M, is a building.

    The state promised $10.42M in cost share beside it. Two of the five slices reach companies directly: Translational R&D, which makes competitive research awards, and Synthe6, a cohort of nine startups. Those two have reached fourteen organizations so far. Trace it on the map

  3. Machine 3 · Good Jobs Challenge (APEX) · $3.08M federal

    This is the second of the two EDA awards. The Greater Akron Chamber signs for it as grantee, and the money funds regional training aimed at 500 enrollments and 320 placements.

    The local match commitment beside it, $3.37M, is larger than the $3.08M award itself. Trace it on the map

Sources: signed federal Notices of Award; the executed Ohio grant agreement (SBIG20251005). An award figure is money a government has signed for, not money it has spent.

What a line item buys

Two award labels carry more than they say

The map gives each award a few words. Two of those labels describe work that a few words cannot hold.

Flexsys · $10.09M · EDA R&D

Replacing the tire additive behind the coho salmon die-offs

Nearly every tire on the road carries 6PPD, an anti-degradant that keeps rubber from cracking. Its breakdown product, 6PPD-quinone, is the chemical a 2021 Science paper identified as killing coho salmon in urban Pacific Northwest streams. Flexsys’s award funds development of a safer alternative in Akron.

See the award

Full Circle Technologies · $4.70M · EDA R&D

Un-making a tire is the hard part

A tire is mostly vulcanized rubber, built never to melt back down, so recycling one into new products is far harder than recycling a bottle. Full Circle’s award funds end-of-life tire recycling, the only recycling project among the Tech Hub’s seven implementation awards.

See the award

The full reconciliation, line by line
Which signed document each figure comes from

    Three machines, one ledger: every dollar of the $106.3 million, awarded and promised, has a line on it.

    Of the $85.3M awarded, $79.2M already has a named recipient, and the largest single line among them, $18.52M, is a building rather than a company. The other $6.17M sits in two Ohio workstreams: $2.64M of Translational R&D not yet sub-granted, and $3.52M running Synthe6 through Bounce Innovation Hub. The two parts are rounded to the nearest hundredth of a million dollars, so they add to $6.16M rather than to the $6.17M total. All of it is awarded money, not money spent.
