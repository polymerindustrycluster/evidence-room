What happened after publication

Almost every month of these three price series moved after publication, by almost nothing.

Every chart on this site shows each month at its latest published value, which hides the Bureau of Labor Statistics revising that figure for months afterwards. This page measures three producer-price series, the prices plants charge for chemicals and allied products and for plastic and rubber goods: 259 of their 273 monthly figures were later changed.

By John Swanson, Polymer Industry Cluster desk · Analysis and graphics by Claude (Anthropic) · Data ALFRED, the federal archive of every version ever published, 2019–2026, newest vintage August 2026

The revisions

Almost every month moved, and almost none moved far

Chemicals and allied products is a broad index, of which resins are one part; the other two price plastic and rubber goods, one product by product and one plant by plant. All three panels share one scale.

Chemicals and allied products typically moves 0.35% after publication, more than twice as far as either of the other two

Each bar is one month. Above the line that month ended higher than the figure first reported, below it lower, and the taller the bar the further it moved. The series that revises hardest is drawn first.

Correction, September 28, 2026: this page previously called series WPU06 “industrial chemicals”. That name belongs to WPU061, a narrower series this page does not use. WPU06 is BLS “Chemicals and allied products”, and every label here now says that.

Correction, September 29, 2026: this page called Chemicals and allied products “the upstream input” to the other two series and placed all three on one supply chain. It is a broad index, of which resins are one part. No number changed.

Correction, September 30, 2026: the opening paragraph said the other two series price goods “made from” chemicals. It now names the three series without that link. No number changed.

Correction, October 5, 2026: the page called each month’s latest published value “today’s”, though the archive was read on 13 August 2026. Those readings are now dated to that vintage, and the chart labels say a month ended higher or lower. No number changed.

The distribution

How far a number typically travels

If the revisions sat mostly on one side, first estimates would be running consistently high or consistently low, and every trend drawn from fresh data would need a warning. They fall close to evenly on both sides, which is the reassuring half of this page.

Half of all revised months land inside 0.15% of the figure first published, and the largest total revision is 1.41%

259 revised months: 137 ended higher than first reported, 121 ended lower, and one came back to within a thousandth of a point of where it started, which rounds to no change at all.

One month, up close

The month that changed direction

On 14 October 2021 the Bureau of Labor Statistics published its first estimate of September prices for chemicals and allied products: an index reading of 348.8, a level that means nothing on its own. The Bureau republished that month three more times, and as of 13 August 2026 it reads 343.866, which is 1.41 percent lower and the largest total revision on this page. It did not arrive at once: the first of the three corrections did almost all of it, taking 348.8 to 343.934 on 14 December 2021, a single step of 1.40 percent, and the two that followed moved the figure 0.04 percent down and 0.02 percent back up. That it moved in more than one step is a change of Bureau policy rather than anything about this month: effective with that same 14 December 2021 release, and starting with the reference months August to October 2021, the Bureau began republishing interim values every month across the three months between a first print and the recalculation four months later, where before it published that recalculation alone. September 2021 sits at the join, its first print before the change and its last correction after, so how many times a month on this page was republished is not comparable across the window. The year-on-year comparison came through it. In the figures a reader had that October, September stood 24.8 percent above September 2020, and in the figures as of 13 August 2026 the same comparison reads 23.1 percent. The month-on-month comparison did not come through it. September read 1.0 percent above August in the October figures, and as of 13 August 2026 it reads 0.3 percent below August. A board deck built that month would have carried the annual figure about right and the monthly one pointing the wrong way.

A fresh figure from these three series is worth quoting for how high it reads, and worth checking against a later version before quoting which way it moved. BLS’s published rule allows revisions for four months after the originally scheduled publication. Its separate five-year seasonal-factor window concerns seasonally adjusted indexes; these three series are unadjusted. Check the current release and any revision notices before reusing an earlier figure. This finite archive cannot guarantee when future revisions stop.
