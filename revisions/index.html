What happened after publication

Almost every month of these three price series moved after publication, by almost nothing.

Every chart on this site shows each month at its current value, which hides the Bureau of Labor Statistics revising that figure for years. The claim here covers three producer-price series and no others, the prices plants charge for chemicals and for the plastic and rubber goods made from them: 259 of 273 published months were later changed, by a typical 0.15 percent of the price first reported.

By John Swanson, Polymer Industry Cluster desk · Analysis and graphics by Claude (Anthropic) · Data ALFRED, the federal archive of every version ever published, 2019–2026 · August 2026

The revisions

Almost every month moved, and almost none moved far

The three series sit on one supply chain. Industrial chemicals is the raw input; rubber and plastic products is the goods made from it, priced product by product; plastics and rubber plants is those same goods priced plant by plant. Industrial chemicals, the series a resin buyer quotes, revises further than the two further down the chain, and all three panels are drawn on the same scale so that comparison can be read straight off them.

Industrial chemicals typically moves 0.35% after publication, more than twice as far as either series downstream of it

One bar per month priced. Bars above the line ended higher than the figure first reported, below it lower, and the taller the bar the further that month moved. Panels run hardest-revising first.

The distribution

How far a number typically travels

If the corrections sat mostly on one side, first estimates would be running consistently high or consistently low, and every trend drawn from fresh data would need a warning. They fall close to evenly on both sides, which is the reassuring half of this page.

Half of all revisions land inside 0.15% of the figure first published, and the largest ever recorded here is 1.41%

259 revised months: 137 ended higher than first reported, 121 ended lower, and one came back to within 0.001 index points of where it started, which rounds to no change at all.

One month, up close

The month that changed direction

On 14 October 2021 the Bureau of Labor Statistics published its first estimate of September industrial chemical prices: an index reading of 348.8, a level that means nothing on its own. Three corrections later that month reads 343.866, or 1.41 percent lower, the largest single revision on this page. A board deck built that October would have carried the annual number right and the monthly one backwards. In the figures a reader had that October, September stood 24.8 percent above September 2020, and today the same comparison reads 23.1 percent. What did not survive is the step: September measured against the month before it.

September against August, as first reported
the same two months, on today’s figures

A fresh figure from these three series is worth quoting for how high it reads, and worth checking against a later version before you quote which way it moved. Nothing in this archive moved more than six months after its first print, so price against the version published half a year later.
