What happened to the numbers after they were published

Almost every month of these three price series moved after publication — by almost nothing.

Every chart on every other page shows the current value of each month. That is a convention, and it hides something: a statistical agency keeps revising a figure for years after it first appears. This page shows the same series as everyone else, plus every value each month has ever had. The scope is three producer-price series, not PIC's figures generally — and the headline finding is that the revisions are small, which is the opposite of what “95% changed” sounds like on its own.

The chart

First published against latest, for every month

One line per month: where the number started and where it ended up. Most move a little. The interesting question is not the size of any single revision but whether they lean — whether first estimates are systematically high or low.

The distribution

How far a number typically travels

Every revised month as one mark, by how much the figure moved between its first appearance and today. If the mass sat cleanly on one side, first estimates would be biased and every trend drawn from fresh data would need a warning.

Showing only the latest vintage is a choice. It is just usually an invisible one.
