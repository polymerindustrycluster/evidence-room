What happened after publication

Almost every month of these three price series moved after publication, by almost nothing.

Every chart on this site shows each month at its current value, which hides the Bureau of Labor Statistics revising that figure for years. The claim here covers three producer-price series and no others: 259 of 273 published months were later changed, by a typical 0.15 percent.

The distribution

How far a number typically travels

Every revised month as one mark, placed by how far the figure moved between its first appearance and today. If the mass sat cleanly on one side, first estimates would be biased and every trend drawn from fresh data would need a warning. It leans, slightly, to the up side.

Half of all revisions land inside 0.15% of the first print, and the largest ever recorded here is 1.42%

259 revised months: 137 ended up, 121 ended down, and one came back to within 0.001 index points of its first print. Left of zero is a downward revision.

One month, up close

The month that changed direction

On 14 October 2021 the Bureau of Labor Statistics published its first estimate of September: industrial chemicals at 348.8. Three corrections later that month reads 343.866, the largest single revision on this page. A board deck built that October would have carried the annual number right and the monthly one backwards. On the vintage a reader held that October, September stood 24.8 percent above September 2020, and today the same comparison reads 23.1 percent. The step from August is the part that did not survive.
