Three polymer program codes in the federal record, 1991–2023

Polymer technician programs were less likely to report completions in 2023 than degrees or the peer trades.

Of 73 polymer technician programs with more than ten recorded lifetime completions, 20 reported a completion in 2023: 27%. Polymer degree programs and six peer technician trades each reached 48% under the same rule. These are historical federal filing records, not a count of training available today. The completions decline measured below runs from the 2016 peak to 2023; the 2023 total remained above the record’s 1991 starting point.

By John Swanson, Polymer Industry Cluster desk · Analysis and graphics by Claude (Anthropic), updated with Codex (OpenAI) · Data IPEDS completions, peer-trade snapshots and College Scorecard · Updated September 2026

The finding

Technician programs reported completions at a lower rate than both comparison groups

The three layers are the polymer technician programs, the polymer degrees above them, and the peer trades taught next door. One rule measures all three over the same years. A program is substantive in this chart if it recorded more than ten lifetime completions, and active means it reported at least one in 2023. Six peer trades provide a comparison: welding, machining, industrial maintenance, manufacturing engineering technology, electrical engineering technology and mechanical engineering technology. The trades and the polymer degrees finish within a point of each other; the technician layer finishes about twenty points below both. One limit belongs with that finding. The polymer codes are the federal catalogue numbers a college puts on a program when it reports completions, and this record sees only what colleges chose to file under them. A program without a 2023 completion may still teach, pause awards, or report under another code. The comparison does not establish stability in either of the other groups.

The record

After a collapse and a recovery, completions are a third below their 2016 peak

Every polymer completion in the record, added up by year, certificates through doctorates. It fell through the 2000s, then more than doubled from the 2007 floor to the 2016 peak. Since that peak, completions have fallen in four of the five years that can be compared with the year before them. The gap in that comparison is 2020: the federal mirror republished 2019’s counts under that year, so this record has no real 2020 to measure against.

The base rate

More than half recorded ten or fewer lifetime completions

The chart above counts only substantive programs. This one drops that filter and looks at every technician program in the record, all 168 of them, to show the small records too. Small means ten or fewer recorded lifetime completions: 95 of 168, or 57%. Brief means a first-to-last reporting span of five years or less, inclusive: 91 of 168, or 54%. Both small and brief describes 75 of 168, or 45%. These overlapping measures describe the record; none establishes that a program failed.

What working looks like

Sixteen technician programs ever passed a hundred completions, and one of them is not like the others

Each bar is one program’s lifetime completions, for the largest eight of the sixteen that ever passed one hundred. Dark bars are still conferring; light bars filed no polymer completion in 2023. Ferris State, a public university in Michigan, is the outlier to study: more completions than the next two programs combined, across 32 counted years from 1991 to 2023, excluding quarantined 2020. Why it persisted while the layer around it thinned is a question this dataset cannot answer. It is the first question to put to the people who run the program.

The anchor

Akron reported 44 completions in the selected polymer codes in 2023; 42 were graduate degrees

The University of Akron’s reported completions fell from 114 in 2016 to 44 in 2023: 16 master’s degrees, 26 doctorates and two bachelor’s degrees. The three selected codes cover Polymer/Plastics Engineering, Polymer Chemistry, and Plastics and Polymer Engineering Technology. They exclude polymer teaching reported under other fields. Most of the decline was in graduate degrees, which still supplied nearly all of Akron’s completions in this record.

This completions series ends in 2023. EDA announced planned Tech Hub implementation funding on July 2, 2024; Ohio’s 2025 annual report (printed page 18) dates the state Innovation Hub announcement to September 2024. Both announcements follow this series, which cannot measure their outcomes.

What it pays

Where the record shows earnings at all, they are strong: $87k–$101k four years out

The College Scorecard, the federal database of what graduates of a given program go on to earn, publishes earnings for bachelor’s degrees under a single federal program code, Polymer/Plastics Engineering, at exactly five institutions. None is in Ohio. They are Penn College, Penn State Behrend, UMass Lowell, Western Washington and the University of Wisconsin-Stout (UW-Stout). The code pools degrees the schools themselves name differently: plastics engineering at UMass Lowell and UW-Stout, engineering technology at Penn College and Penn State Behrend, and at Western Washington a degree since renamed Materials Science & Engineering. Each row shows median earnings one year and four years after graduation. The pale band is the national comparison: the middle half of all programs filed under the same federal code, four years out.

The turn

This record does not establish a verified rebuild

This dataset contains no verified example of a substantial polymer technician program dying and being rebuilt. One case looked like a rebuild. It turned out to be a federal reporting change: the program never stopped teaching, and a renumbering split its record into an ending and a fresh start. That is a reason to verify apparent exits and restarts against program catalogues, not evidence that a future program would be a historical first.

What “rebuild” means depends on which fact you take as the problem: reporting continuity, small and brief records, or the contracting anchor. Each points at a different program design, and the record is compatible with all three readings, which is exactly why the design choice has to be made on purpose.

Polymer technician programs have the lowest 2023 reporting rate in this comparison. Designing new training requires the next check: which apparent exits reflect closure, a changed code, or a different training format? This record cannot answer that alone.
