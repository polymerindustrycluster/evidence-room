Supply against demand

Applications cluster where classified companies are fewest

PIC’s intake form and the vault, the company register PIC keeps with the Greater Akron Chamber, sort companies and projects into the same value-chain stages, so the two can be compared stage by stage. They point opposite ways. Converters and fabricators, the companies that shape polymer into parts, hold twelve classified companies for every application filed in 2026. Raw materials and feedstock runs the other way: one classified company for every two applications, half a company per application. Read that as coverage, not as competition: it says how much of each stage the register has classified against the demand arriving, not how many rivals chase each application, and not capacity, headcount or output. And it counts companies, not size: the 246 converters on the chart below may be small shops, while the twelve companies in raw materials and feedstock may each be a very large plant. The two sides also come from different footprints. Intake uses PIC-12, the twelve counties PIC’s federal-data pages cover; the vault uses NEO-14, the fourteen Northeast Ohio counties drawn on this page. That makes the ratio indicative rather than exact. It is the one place on this site where the two geographies touch, and it is here for that reason.
